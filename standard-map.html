A short explanation

We consider a potential \(V\) of the form:

\[ V(x)=-\frac{K}{2\pi}\cos(2\pi x)-\frac{\varepsilon}{10\pi}\cos(10\pi x). \]

Move the sliders to move parameters \(K\) and \(\varepsilon\).

The corresponding dynamics are given by the 2D map

\[ f(x,y)=\bigl(x+y+V'(x)\pmod 1,\;y+V'(x)\bigr). \]

For a general potential \(V\), the function \(\beta_V(\omega)\) records the smallest average action among orbits with rotation number \(\omega\):

\[ \beta_V\!\left(\frac pq\right)=\frac1q\min_{x_q=x_0+p}\sum_{n=0}^{q-1} \left[\frac12(x_{n+1}-x_n)^2+V(x_n)\right]. \]

Can one deduce \(V\) from \(\beta\)?

This inverse problem, in the spirit of Kac's celebrated question “Can one hear the shape of a drum?”, is treated in this paper.
